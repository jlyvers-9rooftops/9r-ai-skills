# Brief and evidence

## Intake

Normalize the supplied material into these facts; the user's original Markdown
brief is retained at `assets/case-brief.md`.

| Input | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Client / campaign | Exact name, industry/context, websites, project scope |
| Engagement | Start, end or ongoing; distinguish launch and measurement dates |
| Description / challenge | The actual starting situation and business stakes |
| Approach | What 9Rooftops specifically delivered and why |
| Relevance | Intended prospect, RFP criterion, sector, or reusable lesson |
| Unfair advantage | Project-specific differentiator with supporting evidence |
| Results | Outcome, units, baseline/end value, comparison period, source |
| People / partners | Actual personnel and disciplines; subconsultants if involved |
| Branding | Official client palette or supplied brand guidance/assets |
| Use | Leave-behind by default; adapt density for a live presentation when asked |
| Quote | Exact wording, speaker, role, source, and permission to use if relevant |

Do not require an exact start day when only a year is known. Preserve uncertainty
instead of turning “2019” into a fabricated calendar date. Treat missing critical
facts as questions; do not put placeholders or speculative metrics into a final
deck. Omit optional content that is not supplied. If the user requests a draft
with unresolved facts, clearly label it a draft and explain those gaps.

## Source hierarchy and web lookup

1. User-provided approved brief, analytics exports, project documents, and client
   feedback establish what the agency did and what results can be claimed.
2. Supplied existing decks provide historic copy and design precedent. Surface
   contradictions or stale claims; their inclusion does not verify them anew.
3. Limited official-site lookup can establish a current client name, public
   context, website, and brand palette. Cite the exact page/style/brand source and
   access date. Do not substitute agency colors for client colors.
4. Public case pages may corroborate supplied material, but don't infer private
   engagement dates, attribution, analytics, or project personnel from a website.

Ask before broad competitive research or an expanded research assignment. No
external upload, client outreach, or publishing is implied by creating the deck.

## Claim ledger

Maintain a working mapping of claims to sources and dates while drafting. Include
source IDs per slide in the build specification; they become speaker notes.
For a number, capture the metric definition, period, comparison base and source.
A short results footer identifies the source and period; full references and
qualification belong in notes.

Do not confuse percentage change with percentage-point change, traffic share with
traffic volume, delivery scope with business impact, or post-engagement results
with results measured during delivery. Recalculate obvious supplied arithmetic
without replacing authoritative raw data with estimates. Ask about conflicting
figures, comparison dates, and unexplained baselines.

Use qualitative results when actual evidence is qualitative: a documented launch,
unified architecture, established workflow, or validated capability. Do not dress
a deliverable up as conversion lift, revenue, or uptime improvement. Audit and
ranking claims need their tested scope, date, and method. Comparative superiority
and “100%” claims require specific support.

Quotation marks do not create evidence. Use actual attributed quotations only on
the closing slide. Keep agency-written messaging separate from client testimony.

## Audience relevance

For a named prospect or RFP, connect the demonstrated work to the supplied
requirements. If none is given, use a case-grounded transferable lesson, not an
invented prospect profile. Make this relevance explicit in a closing paragraph,
short takeaway, or approach framing; it does not require a sixth slide.
